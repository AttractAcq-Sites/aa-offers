Why this exists

You've paid for marketing before. You got deliverables.

Posts, a report, a dashboard, activity you could not trace to a single customer who paid you. You could not say afterwards what went wrong, only that it did.

Here is the part nobody did: check first whether your offer, your market and your economics could carry paid acquisition at all. They sold the campaign and found out with your money.

Meanwhile the work still arrives — you just can't say why, and you can't make more of it arrive in the month you need it. Referral built this business and referral has flattened. And the crew that's sitting, the empty bay, the benched consultant, the branch with no customers, the new service nobody has bought yet — that costs the same every week whether it bills or not.

The diagnostic answers the question before the money moves: can demand be bought here, for what, at what price, and what would it cost you to find out.

What the diagnostic is

Seven things, written down, about your business.

Not a capability deck. Findings on your market, your competitors, your proof and your numbers — the work a campaign should have been built on the first time.

1

Your market and your customer

Where demand concentrates, what it is searching for and complaining about, and who is actually in a position to buy what you sell.

2

Competitor offers and prices

What is on the table in your market right now and what it costs — so your offer is engineered against reality rather than against a guess.

3

Which of your proof is commercially usable

One recorded proof-extraction session. The job files, the WhatsApp gallery, the eleven-year client, the job nobody else could do. You get back a structured evidence library where every claim is tied to something real in your business. You keep it whatever the verdict.

4

The offer we would engineer

Packaging, specificity, risk reversal, targeting and framing. Cutting your price is not the mechanism, and nothing goes to market without your written sign-off.

5

The total outlay, as one number

Fee and recommended media shown together, because that is how you price the risk — and how your accountant will read it.

6

What idle capacity costs you per week

Worked out from your numbers. The comparison then isn't our fee against nothing. It's our fee against the run rate of the problem.

7

The verdict

Launchable, or not. In writing, with the reasoning attached, in a document you can hand to a partner or an accountant without being in the room.

8

And what it isn't

It is not a forecast. There is no projected lead count, no revenue figure and no return multiple in it, because none of that is knowable before a campaign runs.

Two outcomes. Both of them leave you better off than guessing.

If it's launchable

The diagnostic fee is credited in full against the Offer Launch fee.

If it isn't

We say so, we don't take the campaign, and the fee is refunded in full.

No defensible offer, not enough demand, or economics that don't support the spend — you get that in writing rather than three weeks later with the media budget gone.

The worst outcome available to you here is an honest "don't do this", your money back, and an evidence library you keep.

Two clocks, said plainly

A launch window is a scope boundary and a decision point, not a results deadline. Inside it we are accountable for the nodes we control — offer live, media in market, enquiries captured, conversations opened, appointments booked and shown, each one attributed. Cash collected sits on your sales cycle, which is longer, and we keep measuring past the end date rather than pretending it lands inside the window.

What we will never quote you

A lead volume. A revenue figure. A cost per customer. A return multiple. None of those are knowable before the work runs, and a number invented to close you is a number your accountant will take apart in ten minutes. What we can tell you, before you spend on media, is whether the offer, the market and the economics can carry a launch at all.

Straight answers

The questions you were going to ask anyway.

What do you need from me, and how much of my time is it?

For the diagnostic: a working session on your business, your market and your numbers, and one recorded proof-extraction session. We do the retrieval, the research and the writing. You are not given homework, and you are not asked to brief, curate or produce anything.

Do I have to be on camera?

No. You should not have to become a content creator to grow a service business. Founder, staff, or produced media — how the media gets made is a decision inside the plan, not a condition of buying.

Will you make me discount my service?

No. A stronger offer means better framing, specificity, bundling, risk reversal and sharper targeting. Price reduction is not the default mechanism, and no offer, price or claim goes to market without your written sign-off.

Our customers don't come from Facebook.

They might not. Channel is one of the things the research answers rather than one of the things we assume. If the diagnostic concludes your buyers are not reachable economically on the channels available, that is a reason for the verdict to be no.

What if we can't handle a flood of enquiries?

Then you shouldn't be running a launch, and we'd rather establish that now. Delivery capacity is part of the diagnosis, not a discovery in week two. Selling work you then deliver badly damages the referral reputation that is currently your whole acquisition engine.

My partner and my accountant need to look at it.

Expected. The diagnostic is written to be forwarded and read cold by someone who wasn't on the call: total outlay as one number, the scope, the end point, the refund and credit terms, and what you own at the end.

Do I own what gets built?

The ad account, pixel and campaign data, domain, landing pages, creative, copy, lead data and the evidence library are created in your business's name and stay yours. Where those assets are currently sitting with a former agency, a nephew or a personal profile, we drive the retrieval. The diagnostic itself carries no obligation to continue.
